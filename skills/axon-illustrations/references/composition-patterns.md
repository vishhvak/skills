# Composition patterns

## Structure types (pick one per image)

| type | when the text says | shape on canvas |
|---|---|---|
| two-state contrast | "before vs after", "most people X, but Y" | left/right halves, one thin dividing gap (no drawn line needed) |
| pipeline | "first, then, finally" | 2–4 stations left to right, Axon's tail linking them |
| fork | "two paths", "the tradeoff" | one input splitting into branches; the favored branch carries the orange pulse |
| loop | "compounds", "feedback", "flywheel" | circular flow, Axon completing the circuit with its tail |
| leak / gap | "where it breaks", "the missing piece" | a flow with one visible break; Axon bridging or probing it |
| accumulation | "adds up", "debt", "stack" | items piling into or out of a container Axon is plugged into |
| filter | "signal vs noise", "curation" | many in, few out, Axon as the filtering mechanism |

## Inventing the metaphor (do this fresh, every image)

1. State the sentence the image must argue, in eight words or fewer.
2. Pick the structure type from the table.
3. Choose a physical world for it: kitchen, plumbing, gardening, fishing, weather, machinery, postal. Prefer worlds that make the abstract embarrassing and concrete.
4. Give Axon the working role: what does its tail plug into? Where do the pulses flow?
5. Sanity test: would a reader who skipped the paragraph still get the claim from the image alone? If not, simplify.

## Anti-repetition rules

- Never reuse a metaphor world twice in one article's set.
- Never copy a composition from the examples in this skill or from a previous run; they calibrate density, they are not templates.
- If your first idea is a literal flowchart of the text, discard it and pick a physical world.
