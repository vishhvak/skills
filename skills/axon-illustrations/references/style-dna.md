# Style DNA

The look: a clean sheet of white paper on which someone thoughtful and slightly
unhinged drew one idea with a black pen, then annotated it in two colors.

## Non-negotiables

- **Canvas**: pure white background (#FFFFFF), 16:9 landscape. No texture, no gradient, no vignette.
- **Line work**: black hand-drawn ink lines, confident but imperfect. Single line weight family; slight wobble is good, sketchy scratchiness is not.
- **Fills**: solid black only for Axon and at most one other anchor element. Everything else stays line-drawn and open.
- **Whitespace**: at least a third of the canvas stays empty. If the drawing feels full, the metaphor is doing too much.
- **One structure per image**: one judgment, one flow, one contrast, one loop. Never two.

## Color: exactly two accents

- **Signal orange** — pulses on Axon's tail, motion arrows, the single most important highlight. The "energy" color.
- **Neural blue** — handwritten annotation text and underlines. The "commentary" color.

Black + white + these two. A third color means the image is wrong. Red is
reserved: only for a crossed-out wrong-path element, at most one per image.

## Annotations

- Handwritten English, lowercase, short: 1–4 words per label ("raw notes", "the slow path", "compounds here")
- 2–5 labels per image. Zero is acceptable; six is not.
- Labels point at things with thin blue arrows or sit beside them. Never boxed, never in callout bubbles.
- No title anywhere on the image. The article provides the caption; the image explains, it does not headline.

## Register bans (what this is not)

- PPT/consulting infographics: no icon grids, no 2x2 matrices drawn straight, no pie charts
- Corporate flat illustration, gradients, isometric tech art
- Children's-book cuteness or sticker sheets
- Dense architecture diagrams with 10+ boxes
- Meme formats
